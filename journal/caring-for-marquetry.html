Living with Wood

Caring for a Marquetry Artwork

By Sasun Sargsyan · 25 November 2026 · 3 min read

A marquetry artwork is made of natural wood, finished and sealed, and with a little care it will last for generations. Wood is a living material, though: it responds to light, heat and humidity. These are the few habits that keep it at its best.

1. Mind the light

Light is the slowest but most certain influence on wood. Over the years, ultraviolet light changes the colour of most species: some darken and warm, as cherry does; others lighten, as walnut can; vivid woods gradually soften towards brown. In a marquetry picture, where the image depends on contrast between woods, the aim is to keep those changes slow and even.

  • Hang the work where direct sunlight never falls on it, not even for an hour a day.
  • Prefer LED lighting, which gives off very little ultraviolet or heat. Keep picture lights at a respectful distance.
  • If a room is very bright, consider UV-filtering film on the windows. It protects everything else in the room as well.

2. Keep the air steady

Wood takes up and gives off moisture with the air around it, swelling and shrinking slightly as it does. Sudden or extreme changes are what put stress on veneer and glue.

  • A stable relative humidity, roughly between 40% and 60%, is ideal, and is what most homes already have.
  • Do not hang the work above a radiator or fireplace, beside an air-conditioning vent, or in a bathroom or kitchen.
  • Inside walls are better than cold outside walls, which can be damp behind the frame.

3. Clean gently, and rarely

The surface is sealed, so it needs very little.

  • Dust with a soft, dry microfibre cloth or a soft brush, following the direction of the grain.
  • Never use water, sprays, furniture polish, silicone products or glass cleaner on the surface.
  • If something is spilled on it, blot it immediately with a dry cloth; do not rub.

4. Hang it securely

Use hooks or fixings rated for the weight of the framed work, ideally two points rather than one so it stays level. Hang at eye level, and if possible where the light falls across the surface at an angle: it brings out the grain and the slight changes in texture between woods.

5. Moving and storing

  • Keep the original crate if you can. It is the safest way to move the work.
  • Carry the work upright, by the frame, with two hands.
  • If it must be stored, choose a dry, temperature-stable room, never a garage, loft or basement, and store it upright, not flat under other objects.

If something happens

If a piece of veneer ever lifts, a crack appears, or the surface is damaged, please do not glue or polish it yourself. Keep any loose piece safe and contact the studio: describe what happened and send a photograph, and we will advise you on the right way to restore it.

Treated with this small amount of care, a marquetry portrait does not simply survive. It mellows, as fine wood does, and becomes more beautiful with time.
